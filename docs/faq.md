# Practical questions

## Who is StationOS for?

Businesses that want a station shaped around their audience, and independent radio makers, collectors and communities who want to share a musical identity. Shops, hospitality venues and shared spaces can choose self-hosting or discuss Hosted and Fully managed services.

## Does it include music?

Self-hosted StationOS connects to your own compatible UPnP sources. Audio stays on those servers. Hosted uses your own music sources; Fully managed includes music sourcing to an agreed brief. Commercial service details are agreed separately.

## What does it cost?

The software is open source under GPL-3.0-only. Self-hosters provide hosting, music sources and any AI services. Text, speech and image services can have separate charges. Visit [StationOS](https://stationos.cloud/#options) to discuss commercial scope and pricing.

## Are the DJs real people?

No. They are fictional presenter profiles with generated speech. You set their personality, editorial direction and voice. Review and refine those choices to suit your audience.

## Can I listen without configuring AI?

Yes. Start with a station, a playable default playlist and a reachable music source. AI services add production assistance, curation and generated presentation.

## Does it broadcast all the time?

Production follows listeners. The first connection starts the shared programme; the last disconnection stops it. A weekly schedule determines which show applies when listeners tune in. The default playlist covers gaps.

## Does everyone hear the same thing?

Everyone joins one shared programme. Different device buffers can create a small audible delay between players. StationOS is not a synchronised multi-room speaker system.

## Can I run several stations?

Yes. Each has its own identity, playlists, schedule and stream address, using shared sources and DJ profiles. Capacity depends on your server, AI services and available bandwidth.

## What if an AI service fails?

Unavailable curation or speech can fall back to eligible music while preparation retries. A playable source and pool are still required. Runtime status and Incidents help you identify faults.

## Can I include podcasts, audiobooks and advertising?

Yes. Ordered episodes keep podcast instalments and audiobook chapters in their intended sequence. Advertising slots make room for promotions, sponsor messages and commercial partners within your programme.

## Where do I report a software problem?

Open an issue in the repository with the observed behaviour, what you expected and relevant redacted logs. Keep passwords, API keys, private music URLs and database files out of public reports. For an agreed commercial service, use its support route.
